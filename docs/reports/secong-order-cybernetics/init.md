# Context Mechanics

Context mechanics is the systematic study of how humans and AI systems work together effectively, from simple information exchange to complex collaborative work. 
It aims to investigate the interaction processes that enable effective human-AI integration. Rather than treating AI as sophisticated tooling, it studies how different cognitive architectures coordinate attention, build shared understanding, and achieve enhanced outcomes through systematic collaboration.

## Key Concepts

### The Context Window as Fundamental Unit
The context window provides the primary empirical phenomenon for study because it is:
- **Observable** from both human and AI perspectives
- **Measurable** through length, complexity, and coherence metrics  
- **Manipulable** for controlled experimentation
- **Consequential** for collaboration effectiveness

### Attention Types
- **Human attention**: Sequential, selective, assumption-filling, goal-directed
- **AI attention**: Parallel, comprehensive, pattern-completing, weight-distributed
- **Interface-mediated attention**: Tool calls, approval points, shared focus moments

### Differing Perspectives as Driving Force
The systematic use of AI's different observational and analytical capabilities to study collaborative dynamics from multiple angles. Unlike "AI phenomenology" (which requires unresolved theoretical frameworks), AI perspectivism requires only that AI systems provide systematically different perspectives - not consciousness or subjective experience.

## Focus Areas
- **Information Flow**: How context develops, degrades, and transforms during human-AI interaction
- **Attention Coordination**: How different processing styles (human: sequential, selective; AI: parallel, weighted) create shared focus
- **Integration Spectrum**: From simple Q&A to complex collaborative work, with particular interest in how novel insights develop at the high end
- **Real-Time Dynamics**: How coordinative patterns evolve during actual work sessions

## Origins: From Engineering to Science

Context mechanics emerged organically from intensive context engineering practice during complex AI implementation projects. What started as applied problem-solving gradually revealed patterns and principles suggesting a broader scientific research program.

**The Evolution:**
1. **Context Engineering Practice** → Systematic methods for effective human-AI collaboration
2. **Methodological Innovation** → Tool Approval/Refusal Protocol (TARP) enabling real-time study of coordination
3. **Scientific Recognition** → Understanding that collaboration could be studied while doing collaboration
4. **Research Program** → Context mechanics as foundational science for human-AI interaction processes

## Methodological Principles

**Perspectival Bracketing**: We remain agnostic about consciousness and subjective experience for both human and AI participants, focusing instead on observable differences in information processing and analytical capabilities that can be systematically leveraged for integrative advantage.

**Biological Grounding**: We draw analogies and frameworks from evolutionary biology, where inter-entity integration, mutual negotiation, and symbiotic relationships have been tested across vast timescales. Patterns from quorum sensing, slime mold networks, mycorrhizal associations, and other biological coordination systems provide empirically grounded models for understanding human-AI interaction dynamics without requiring novel theoretical commitments.

**Self-Referential Research**: The project studies human-AI collaboration while actively using it as its primary research method. The method aims to engage self-reference and re-entry in productive ways.

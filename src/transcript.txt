An important concept in this project on collaborative context engineering is the differing perspectives on the context window. 

Humans tend to view the context window as a linear sequence of messages that recedes into the past; each new message tends to make older messages less relevant and less salient.

For AI, the entire context window is embedded and run through many layers of attention for each message; everything is reprocessed and reinterpreted in sequence. 

I'm sure I'm missing some details and nuances, but the high-level feels about right to me. What are your thoughts on this?

Edit
You've captured something really important here. The fundamental asymmetry between human and AI context processing creates fascinating collaboration dynamics that I think have broader implications.

Your framing is largely accurate - humans do experience context as a temporal flow where recency creates salience, while AI processes the entire context window as a simultaneous multidimensional space. But there are some interesting nuances worth exploring:

Human Context Processing:

Not just linear decay, but selective attention patterns - humans actively filter and prioritize based on relevance to current goals
Chunking and hierarchical organization - we naturally group related information and create mental models
Emotional and experiential weighting - some information "sticks" better based on personal significance or surprise
Context switching costs - humans struggle when topics shift rapidly or when managing multiple concurrent threads
AI Context Processing:

While everything gets reprocessed, attention mechanisms do create differential weighting - not all tokens are equally influential
Positional effects still matter - earlier context can get "washed out" by later patterns, even if it's technically still present
Semantic interference - when topics mix in complex ways, the multidimensional processing can create the "chaotic mass of weird topics" effect you mentioned in your documents
The Collaboration Insight: This creates a unique partnership opportunity where human selective attention can help AI maintain focus, while AI's comprehensive processing can help humans avoid blind spots. The "tool approval/refusal protocol" you developed essentially creates synchronization points where both processing styles can align.

What's particularly interesting is that this mismatch explains why traditional "context engineering" (just better prompts) often fails - it's trying to solve an architectural difference with content changes. The multi-perspective approach you've documented seems to work because it leverages both processing styles rather than fighting them.
